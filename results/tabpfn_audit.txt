    an inelegance, not a defect, and the case for the scoring rules
    rests on propriety instead.

  The directional constructions read the sign of the shift; the
  proper scoring rules read its magnitude, so they cannot separate
  an imminent upgrade from an imminent downgrade and their two AUCs
  sit on the same side of one half.  That is why Section 4.4 tests
  them against the one-sided alternative alone.

==============================================================================
A4  sign convention of the stored PIT
==============================================================================
  Every script stores within_class_std_rank as
      rank(directional_deviation, ascending=False) / count,
  and DD = expected_rating - actual_rating with 1=AAA .. 6=B, so a bond
  the model thinks is WORSE than its label has DD > 0 and lands near 0.
  Meeting 7 §3.1.1 states the opposite ('downgrade -> PIT near 1').
  Measured, over every configuration above: AUC(PIT -> downgrade) < 0.5
  and AUC(PIT -> upgrade) > 0.5, confirming the stored PIT is inverted
  relative to the write-up.  One-sided E-values built on it fire on the
  wrong tail; the fix is ascending=True, or 1 - Z downstream.
